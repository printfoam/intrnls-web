Why we started.

The honest version, without the origin myth.

01

The short version

We got tired of good engineering that never made it out of the lab. The physics would work, the prototype would work, and then the project would end at a report because nobody was set up to carry it into a plant. So we built a company around the whole arc — material, design, pilot, product — and decided we would rather do that four times than do the first half forty times.

We think shape is the most under-used tool in industry. Geometry can do what chemistry, energy, and mass used to do — hold a film, move heat, carry a load — if someone is willing to design it from the physics up and then make it at scale. Most people aren’t willing. That’s the gap.

For a client it means we say no a lot, ask uncomfortable questions early, and stay through the part where it gets hard. It also means that when we do take something on, we intend to finish it — in your plant, not in a report.

How we work

Three things we hold to.

Each of these has cost us an engagement at some point. That’s how we know they’re principles and not slogans — a principle with no cost is a tagline.

01

Big problems only

If it won’t change how something is built or run at scale, we don’t take it. We have turned away good money because it was small — and we’d do it again.

02

All the way to the plant

We don’t stop at the prototype. Pilot and commercialization are part of the job, and we have lost engagements by insisting on them. We insist anyway, because that’s where it counts.

03

Physics first, then opinion

Every claim we make has a measurement behind it or it doesn’t leave the building. That slows us down, it occasionally embarrasses us, and it’s why partners trust our numbers.
