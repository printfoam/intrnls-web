Evaporative media

Cooling towers and evaporative air coolers both cool with a wet material called evaporative media.

Air moves through the wet media, and a little of the water evaporates. That carries the heat away. In a cooling tower the media is called fill. In an air cooler it is called a pad. We developed a new manufacturing platform that makes both.

Cooling towers

A cooling tower cools water by letting a little of it evaporate.

A cooling tower cools water for a building or a plant. Warm water pours down through the tower while big fans move air through it. A little of the water evaporates, and that carries the heat away. On the way down, the water runs over the fill, which spreads it out so the air can touch as much of it as possible.

The problem

The fill is what the fans work against, every hour the tower runs.

The harder it is for air to get through the fill, the more work the fans do. Over the life of a tower, that adds up to a lot of electricity.

Does it fit

You keep the tower. We change what is inside it.

It is a drop-in replacement for the fill. It handles the same air and water, and the fans do less work to move the air through it. Whether it suits your tower is the first thing we will talk through.

How we do it

We develop the material, design the shape and test it.

Geometry is the shape of the media. Ours is a lattice: an open, repeating 3D pattern that the water runs over and the air moves through.
